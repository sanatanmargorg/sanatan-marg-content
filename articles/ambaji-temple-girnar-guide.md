Ambaji Temple on Girnar is a hill shrine dedicated to Ambaji Mata, also known as Amba Mata, in Junagadh, Gujarat. It stands on Mount Girnar above Bhavnath Taleti and can be reached by a long stair climb or, when services are running, by ropeway followed by walking. The shrine is part of a larger Girnar pilgrimage area, so plan for both darshan and the physical effort of the hill.

## Where is Ambaji Temple on Girnar?

Ambaji Temple is on Mount Girnar in Junagadh, Gujarat, above Bhavnath Taleti. Visitors reach it by stairs or, when available, by ropeway followed by walking.

The temple is not the same as the famous Ambaji temple in the town of Ambaji in Banaskantha district. Both shrines are dedicated to the Goddess, but they are in different parts of Gujarat. On maps and local signs, the Girnar shrine may appear as Ambaji Mata Temple or Ambaji Temple, Girnar.

Girnar is more than one shrine. The hill has important places linked with Hindu, Jain and other religious traditions. Many pilgrims visit Ambaji Mata and continue towards other sacred points, depending on their time, health and purpose.

## Why is Ambaji Mata worshipped at Girnar?

Ambaji Mata is worshipped as a form of the Divine Mother. Devotees come to offer prayers, seek inner strength and remember the Goddess during a demanding pilgrimage. The climb itself can become part of the prayer when taken with patience and care.

There is no single prayer that every visitor must recite. You may offer a simple namaskar, repeat a name of the Goddess, sit quietly for a few minutes or follow the temple’s customary worship. If you visit with family members from different traditions, a quiet and respectful prayer is enough.

The wider Girnar area has its own rich religious setting. Keep your attention on the shrine you are visiting, and do not treat other temples, images or rituals as tourist displays.

## How do you reach Ambaji Temple from Junagadh?

Most visitors first travel to Junagadh and then go towards Bhavnath Taleti, the usual starting area for the Girnar ascent. Junagadh is connected by road and rail, while local autos, taxis and other transport can help with the final trip to the hill base. Check current local transport details before leaving, as routes and availability can change.

### Climbing by stairs

The traditional route is a long stair climb from the base. It takes physical effort and can feel harder in heat, humidity or crowded conditions. Start early if the route is open and safe, take regular rests and do not rush to keep pace with other pilgrims.

Older adults, young children and anyone with a health condition should think about the climb before starting. A ropeway may reduce the uphill effort, but it does not turn the visit into a drive-up darshan.

### Using the ropeway

The Girnar ropeway can take visitors close to the Ambaji level when it is operating. You may still need to walk and use steps after the ride. Tickets, queues, operating hours, weather restrictions and maintenance closures can change, so check current information on the day of your visit.

| Travel option | Good for | What to plan for |
|---|---|---|
| Stairs from Bhavnath Taleti | A traditional full pilgrimage | A long ascent, heat and regular rest breaks |
| Ropeway followed by walking | Reducing the uphill climb | Tickets, queues and steps after the ride |
| Local transport to Bhavnath Taleti | Reaching the hill base | The shrine itself is not reached by road vehicle |

## What should you carry and wear for the visit?

A little preparation makes the climb safer and the darshan more peaceful. Carry only what you need, since a heavy bag becomes tiring on steps.

- Wear comfortable walking shoes for the approach. Be ready to remove footwear wherever temple rules require it.
- Carry drinking water and simple food if permitted on the route. Use bins and take all plastic back with you when needed.
- Wear clean, modest clothing suitable for a place of worship and a long walk.
- Carry sun protection, a small first-aid kit and any regular medicines you need.
- Keep your phone and wallet secure in crowded areas.
- Check weather, temple access and ropeway status before setting out.

Avoid loud music, littering, pushing in queues and taking photographs where photography is restricted. Do not feed monkeys or leave food near shrines. Follow signs and instructions from temple staff, especially during busy festival periods.

## How can you plan a respectful darshan?

Try to reach with enough time to slow down before entering the shrine. Wash your hands when facilities are available, dress properly and keep your voice low. If there is a queue, stand in line rather than trying to move ahead for a faster darshan.

Temple timings and special arrangements may differ on ordinary days, weekends and festivals. Do not rely on an old travel post for exact hours. Confirm the current schedule locally, and allow extra time for queues, rest and the return route.

You can keep your offering simple. Flowers, fruit or another offering may be accepted according to the temple’s rules, but offerings are not a condition for sincere prayer. Ask before offering anything unfamiliar, and do not place items on the deity or altar unless directed to do so.

## Which mantras can you chant at Girnar?

A mantra should support attention and devotion, not become a race to complete a count. Choose a prayer you know, pronounce it with care and repeat it slowly. At the shrine, follow any local guidance about chanting aloud.

### Radhe Radhe naam japa

Radhe Radhe is a simple devotional repetition associated with loving remembrance of Radha and Krishna. Devotees may find that repeating it gently steadies the mind, supports concentration and brings a feeling of closeness in prayer. These are spiritual benefits, not a guaranteed cure or promise of material results.

You can repeat Radhe Radhe while sitting quietly, walking a safe stretch of the path or praying at home. For a fuller explanation of the practice, read [Radhe Radhe: Meaning, Benefits and Japa](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/radhe-radhe-mantra-guide).

### Vishnu Gayatri and Shiva Gayatri

Some devotees also recite these traditional Gayatri prayers:

**Om Narayanaya Vidmahe Vasudevaya Dhimahi Tanno Vishnu Prachodayat**

This is a prayer for contemplation of Narayana and Vasudeva, asking Vishnu to guide the mind and understanding.

**Om Tatpurushaya Vidmahe Mahadevaya Dhimahi Tanno Rudrah Prachodayat**

This is a prayer for contemplation of Mahadeva, asking Rudra to guide the mind and inner awareness.

Transliteration and pronunciation can vary. Learn the wording from a trusted teacher or a reliable devotional source, and keep the practice calm and sincere. You do not need to chant every mantra during one visit.

## Is this the Ambaji Temple in Banaskantha or Amarkantak?

No. Ambaji Temple on Girnar is in Junagadh, Gujarat. The well-known Ambaji temple in Banaskantha is a separate shrine, and Amarkantak is a different pilgrimage town in Madhya Pradesh.

If you mean the main sacred temple at Amarkantak, the answer depends on the shrine. At the Narmada Udgam Temple, the principal sacred presence is Goddess Narmada, while Amarkantak also has temples dedicated to Shiva and other deities. Saying only Amarkantak temple can cause confusion, so use the full temple name when planning a visit.

The same care helps with Girnar. Say Ambaji Temple, Girnar, Junagadh when asking for directions, booking local transport or checking opening information.

## How can you continue the practice at home?

Before travelling, read about the shrine, choose one short prayer and set a simple intention for the visit. After returning, spend a few minutes in gratitude instead of treating the pilgrimage as finished the moment you leave the hill. A small daily practice is often easier to keep than a long routine done only once.
