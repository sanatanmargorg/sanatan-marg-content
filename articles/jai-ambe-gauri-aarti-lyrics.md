## What Are the Jai Ambe Gauri Lyrics?

Jai Ambe Gauri is a traditional Hindi aarti sung in praise of Maa Durga, Amba, and Gauri. Below is a commonly sung Roman-script version, followed by its simple meaning and a guide for singing it at home.

### Traditional Jai Ambe Gauri aarti lyrics

Small changes in spelling and wording are common across regions, families, and temples. The following version is widely sung:

Jai Ambe Gauri

Ujjwal Se Do Naina, Chandravadan Niko  
Jai Ambe Gauri

Raktpushp Gal Mala, Kanthhar Saje  
Jai Ambe Gauri

Jai Ambe Gauri

Kotik Chandra Divakar, Rajat Jyoti  
Jai Ambe Gauri

Jai Ambe Gauri

## What Does This Aarti Mean?

The aarti praises Devi as the loving Mother of the world and as the powerful force that removes fear and suffering. It combines descriptions of her beautiful form with stories of her victory over harmful forces.

The opening names Ambe, Gauri, and Shyama. It says that Hari, Brahma, and Shiva meditate on her. The verses then describe her sindoor, tilak, bright eyes, moon-like face, red clothing, flowers, ornaments, and radiant light.

The lion, sword, and khappar show different sides of Devi. The lion represents courage, while the weapons show her power to protect dharma and destroy evil. The verses about Shumbha, Nishumbha, Mahishasura, Dhumralochana, Chanda, Munda, Raktabija, Madhu, and Kaitabha refer to well-known Devi narratives.

| Part of the aarti | Main idea |
|---|---|
| Opening refrain | Praise of Amba, Gauri, and Shyama |
| Description of her form | Beauty, auspiciousness, light, and grace |
| Lion and weapons | Courage, protection, and the defeat of harmful forces |
| Demon-slaying verses | Devi's victory over forces that threaten dharma |
| Mother and final prayer | Relief from suffering, blessings, peace, and well-being |

The final verses ask for happiness, prosperity, and the fulfilment of sincere wishes. Devotees generally understand these blessings as the grace and support needed to live with faith and strength, not as a promise of instant wealth or a replacement for practical effort.

## How Can You Sing Jai Ambe Gauri at Home?

You can sing this aarti during a morning or evening puja, on Navratri, or whenever you wish to offer prayer to Devi. A simple, clean space and a sincere mind are enough; a large ritual setup is not required.

### A simple home method

1. Wash your hands and sit in a clean place before an image or murti of Devi, if you have one.
2. Light a diya only on a stable, fire-safe surface. Incense, flowers, and a small water offering are optional.
3. Begin with a quiet prayer and sing the refrain slowly.
4. Continue through the stanzas, keeping your attention on Devi rather than trying to sing quickly.
5. Offer the aarti flame safely at the end, if this is part of your family practice.
6. Sit quietly for a moment and offer a personal prayer for wisdom, courage, and the welfare of all.

Aarti is often a shared practice, so it is fine to sing with family members or follow a temple rendition. If you are alone, reading the words with devotion is also a meaningful form of prayer.

### Pronunciation and common variations

Roman spellings cannot show every Hindi sound. For example, Ambe may appear as Ambey, Nishumbh as Nishumbha, and Raktpushp as Rakt Pushpa. Some versions also begin with Om Jai Ambe Gauri instead of Jai Ambe Gauri.

Do not feel anxious if your family sings one line differently. Learn the version used by your home or temple, and check the meaning of any unfamiliar line. Singing with care is better than rushing to copy every sound perfectly.

## Is This Aarti Connected With Other Devi Mantras?

Jai Ambe Gauri is an aarti, not a Gayatri mantra, but it shares the same spirit of devotion found in many forms of Devi worship. It can be sung on its own or included in a longer puja.

The line Ya Devi Sarva Bhuteshu honours the Divine Mother who is present in all beings. Traditional recitations describe her as present through qualities such as awareness, strength, peace, and compassion. Devotees may use this mantra to remember the sacred value of life and to bring a calm, respectful attitude to daily actions.

The [Durga Gayatri Mantra guide](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/durga-gayatri-mantra-guide) explains a different form of prayer to Devi. The Shiva Gayatri, commonly recited as Om Tatpurushaya Vidmahe, Mahadevaya Dhimahi, Tanno Rudrah Prachodayat, is addressed to Shiva. Its simple sense is: we meditate on Mahadeva and pray that divine wisdom guide our understanding.

The Vishnu Gayatri, often recited as Om Narayanaya Vidmahe, Vasudevaya Dhimahi, Tanno Vishnu Prachodayat, is addressed to Narayana or Vasudeva. It asks Vishnu to inspire and guide the mind. These mantras are related through devotion, but they are not lines from the Jai Ambe Gauri aarti.

## Can You Sing It at an Ambaji Temple?

Yes. Ambaji is a beloved name of Devi, and this aarti may be heard in Devi temples and homes. The exact aarti used at a temple can vary with local custom, so visitors should follow the prayer being sung there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Jai Ambe Gauri the same as a Durga aarti?

Yes. It is a widely sung aarti addressed to Amba, Gauri, and other forms of Devi. Local versions may use small changes in spelling or wording.

### Can I sing it without knowing every word?

Yes. Sing slowly, learn one stanza at a time, and focus on the prayer's feeling and meaning. Listening to a trusted temple or family rendition can help with pronunciation.

### What does Ya Devi Sarva Bhuteshu mean?

It means the Devi who is present in all beings. The mantra honours the Divine Mother as the power that appears in many forms, such as awareness, strength, peace, and compassion.

### Are the Jai Ambe Gauri lyrics the same everywhere?

No. The main stanzas are widely shared, but Roman spellings, line order, and a few words can differ by region and singer. Use the version followed by your family or temple, and compare the meaning if a line sounds different.
