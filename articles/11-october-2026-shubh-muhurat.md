## What Is Auspicious on 11 October 2026?

11 October 2026 falls on Sunday and marks Ashwin Shukla Pratipada, the first day of Sharad Navratri in the usual Indian calendar. The main auspicious observance is Ghatasthapana, which is generally done in the morning after sunrise; the exact shubh muhurat depends on your city, sunrise, and local panchang.

Here is the quick view:

| Item | Practical detail |
|---|---|
| Date | Sunday, 11 October 2026 |
| Tithi | Ashwin Shukla Pratipada, subject to local sunrise and tithi timings |
| Main occasion | First day of Sharad Navratri and Ghatasthapana |
| Preferred period | Morning after sunrise, with the local Ghatasthapana muhurat |
| Exact clock time | Check a panchang set to your city |

Ghatasthapana is the ritual of placing and worshipping the kalash at the start of Navratri. It is not the same as a general muhurat for every activity. A time suitable for kalash installation should not automatically be used for marriage, house entry, a vehicle purchase, or another major ceremony.

## How Do You Find the Exact Shubh Muhurat for Your City?

There is no single India-wide clock time for 11 October 2026. Sunrise changes from one place to another, and panchangs may also show different tithi boundaries based on the location and tradition selected.

Use these steps:

1. Select your city or the place where the puja will take place.
2. Confirm the local sunrise and the date on which Pratipada is present.
3. Look for the Ghatasthapana or Navratri Kalash Sthapana section.
4. Choose the listed morning muhurat rather than copying a time from another city.
5. Check Rahu Kaal and other unsuitable periods if your family follows those rules.

The morning is preferred because Ghatasthapana is traditionally completed early in the day. If the listed period is short, prepare everything before it begins. Keep the kalash, water, mango leaves or other customary items, grains, lamp, flowers, and prayer material ready in advance.

## What Should You Do During the Navratri Puja?

A simple home puja can be done with care and a clean heart. Follow your family tradition if it differs from this general outline.

### Prepare the place

Clean the puja area and place a cloth or small platform. Keep the image or murti of the Devi in a respectful position. Avoid placing ritual items where they may be disturbed during the day.

### Set the sankalp and kalash

Sit facing a suitable direction, state your intention in simple words, and place the kalash according to your family practice. Many households use water, grains, leaves, a coconut, and a sacred thread, but local customs vary.

### Offer prayer

Light a lamp, offer flowers and water, and pray to Durga or the form of Devi worshipped by your family. You may recite a familiar Devi mantra, Durga stotra, or a short prayer in your own language. Regular, attentive worship matters more than using a long list of offerings.

### Close the puja calmly

Offer naivedya, perform aarti if that is part of your practice, and sit quietly for a few minutes. Keep the kalash in place for the period observed by your household, then complete the visarjan or closing ritual in the usual way.

## Which Mantras Fit This Day?

The main focus on 11 October is Devi worship because it begins Sharad Navratri. You can also include Ganesha prayer before starting and a personal mantra that supports your daily practice.

### Om Hanumate Namah

Om Hanumate Namah means “salutations to Hanuman” or “I bow to Hanuman.” It is a simple prayer connected with Hanuman’s courage, strength, service, and devotion. You can chant it on Sunday if it is part of your practice; it does not replace the Devi worship that marks this Navratri day. Read [Om Hanumate Namah: Benefits and Daily Practice](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/om-hanumate-namah-benefits) for a clearer explanation.

### Ganesha Gayatri mantra

The Ganesha Gayatri is a prayer asking Ganesha to guide and illumine the mind. Families and traditions may use slightly different wording, so learn the full mantra from a trusted source and pronounce it with care. The [Ganesha Gayatri Mantra: Meaning & Practice](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/ganesha-gayatri-mantra-meaning) guide explains its purpose in simple terms.

## What Was the Tithi on 24 September 2026?

24 September 2026 falls on a Thursday. In the usual lunar sequence for this period, it is associated with Shukla Trayodashi around sunrise in many Indian calendars, although the exact tithi shown for a local date depends on the panchang and the time boundary.

This is a useful reminder: a date alone does not tell you the full muhurat. The tithi, weekday, nakshatra, sunrise, Rahu Kaal, and the purpose of the ceremony all matter. If you are comparing 24 September with 11 October, do not carry over the timing from one date to the other.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

A few simple checks prevent most problems with a festival muhurat:

- Do not use a timing copied from Delhi, Mumbai, or another city unless the puja is taking place there.
- Do not assume Pratipada or another tithi lasts from midnight to midnight.
- Do not confuse the Ghatasthapana period with a general time for every auspicious activity.
- Do not begin in a rush. Arrange the puja items before the selected period.
- Do not let a timing question create fear. Muhurat supports discipline, but sincere prayer and respectful conduct remain central.

If a family priest has given your household a specific method, follow that guidance. Regional customs for kalash placement, food, fasting, and the length of Navratri worship can be different and still be valid within their tradition.
