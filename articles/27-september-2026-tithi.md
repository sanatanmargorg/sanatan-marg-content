For most of India, 27 September 2026 falls on Krishna Paksha Pratipada, the first lunar day after Bhadrapada Purnima. In a Purnimanta calendar it is usually called Ashwin Krishna Pratipada; in an Amanta calendar it is Bhadrapada Krishna Pratipada. The exact clock time for the tithi changes by location, so use your city and local sunrise for a final check.

## What Is the Tithi on 27 September 2026?

The tithi on Sunday, 27 September 2026 is Krishna Paksha Pratipada. It is the first tithi of the waning half of the lunar month and begins after the full-moon tithi has ended.

| Calendar detail | Answer |
|---|---|
| Gregorian date | Sunday, 27 September 2026 |
| Paksha | Krishna Paksha, the waning half |
| Tithi | Pratipada, the first tithi |
| Lunar phase before it | Bhadrapada Purnima |
| Month name | Ashwin in Purnimanta calendars; Bhadrapada in Amanta calendars |
| Exact clock time | Depends on city, time zone, and local sunrise |

A panchang may show the tithi as Pratipada at sunrise and list the time it ends later that day. That end time is useful if you are choosing a muhurta, planning a vrata, or following a family custom.

## Why Does the Tithi Change at a Different Time from Midnight?

A tithi is based on the distance between the Sun and Moon, not on the midnight-to-midnight clock. Each tithi covers 12 degrees of this movement, so it can begin in the morning, afternoon, night, or at any other hour.

This creates a common mistake. The Gregorian date stays fixed from midnight, but a Hindu calendar usually names the day's tithi by the tithi present at local sunrise. The same tithi can therefore be shown on different civil dates in places far apart.

For 27 September 2026, Indian panchangs read the day as Krishna Pratipada. Someone outside India should check the local sunrise and the tithi end time for their own city. Do not copy an India timing without checking the time zone.

## Why Do Some Calendars Say Ashwin and Others Say Bhadrapada?

Both names can be correct because Hindu lunar months are counted in more than one calendar tradition.

### Purnimanta calendar

In a Purnimanta system, the month ends with the full moon. The Krishna Paksha that follows Bhadrapada Purnima is counted as part of Ashwin. In this system, 27 September 2026 is called Ashwin Krishna Pratipada.

### Amanta calendar

In an Amanta system, the month ends with Amavasya, or the new moon. The waning fortnight before that new moon remains part of Bhadrapada. This gives the name Bhadrapada Krishna Pratipada.

The tithi is still Pratipada in both systems. The difference is the month label, not a disagreement about the lunar day. Regional calendars, family traditions, and festival customs may use one system more often than the other.

## How Can You Check the Exact Tithi for Your City?

Use a location-based panchang rather than a general date list. Follow these steps:

1. Select your city or the nearest major city.
2. Choose 27 September 2026 in the Hindu calendar or panchang section.
3. Check the tithi shown at sunrise.
4. Read the tithi start and end times in your local time zone.
5. If you are observing a fast or selecting a muhurta, follow the rule given by your family tradition or local priest.

The main details to compare are the sunrise time, tithi name, tithi ending time, and the calendar system used. A page that only says Pratipada but gives no location may not answer the practical question for your home.

Do not treat Krishna Pratipada as a universal fast day. Pratipada itself does not require everyone to fast. A vrata may be connected with a particular deity, regional custom, or family practice, so check the full rule before changing your food or daily routine.

## What Puja Can You Do on Krishna Pratipada?

There is no single puja that every Hindu must perform on Krishna Pratipada. A simple prayer at home is suitable if you want to mark the day.

You can:

- Clean your prayer space and light a diya safely.
- Offer water, flowers, or fruit according to your custom.
- Pray to your chosen deity with a calm mind.
- Read a short stotra, scripture passage, or prayer.
- Recite a familiar mantra for a few minutes.
- Practise kindness, self-control, and remembrance through the day.

The value of the practice comes from attention and sincerity, not from forcing a long ritual. If you are new to tithi-based observance, a short daily puja is enough. You can also learn more from this guide to [morning puja at home](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/morning-puja-at-home).

### Optional mantra choices

If your devotion is directed towards Devi, `Ya Devi Sarva Bhuteshu` is a meaningful prayer. Its central idea is to honour the Divine Mother as present in all beings. Devotees may find that reciting it supports gratitude, respect, and awareness of the sacred in daily life. These are spiritual aims, not guaranteed material results.

For Vishnu worship, the mantra `Om Narayanaya Vidmahe Vasudevaya Dhimahi Tanno Vishnu Prachodayat` expresses meditation on Narayana and Vasudeva and a prayer for clear guidance. For Shiva worship, `Om Tatpurushaya Vidmahe Mahadevaya Dhimahi Tanno Rudrah Prachodayat` is used as a prayer for remembrance, steadiness, and inner clarity.

You do not need to recite all three mantras. Choose the prayer that fits your family practice and learn the pronunciation carefully. A regular, respectful practice is better than rushing through many chants.

## Can This Tithi Guide a Temple or Pilgrimage Visit?

It can help you choose the spiritual mood or prayer for the day, but the tithi does not set temple opening hours. Temple timings, special pujas, crowd levels, and local rules must be checked separately before travelling.

If you are planning a visit to Amarkantak Temple in Madhya Pradesh, see the [Amarkantak Temple visit guide](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/amarkantak-temple-main-deity-guide). Check the temple's current local information as well as the panchang, especially if you want to attend a specific puja.

A tithi also does not automatically decide whether a wedding, house ceremony, or other major event is suitable. Such decisions need a full muhurta review, including nakshatra, yoga, karana, location, and the purpose of the ceremony.

## How Should You Check Another Date, Such as 23 August 2026?

Treat 23 August 2026 as a separate panchang question. Tithis do not follow the same Gregorian date every year, and the tithi at sunrise can differ from the tithi later that day.

Enter 23 August 2026 and your city into a reliable panchang, then read the tithi at sunrise and its ending time. Do not carry over the Krishna Pratipada result from 27 September. The two dates belong to different points in the lunar cycle.
