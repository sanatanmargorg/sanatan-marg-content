## What Does a Tithi Calendar Show?

A tithi calendar shows Hindu lunar days, festival dates, fasting days and key timings for a chosen place. Unlike a Gregorian calendar, it follows the relationship between the Sun and Moon, so a tithi can start or end during a civil date and an observance may depend on sunrise.

## How Do You Read a Tithi Calendar?

A tithi is a lunar day. It is based on the changing distance between the Sun and Moon, not on midnight-to-midnight dates. A lunar month has 30 tithis, divided into two pakshas, or halves.

### Shukla Paksha and Krishna Paksha

Shukla Paksha is the bright, waxing half of the lunar month. Krishna Paksha is the dark, waning half. Each paksha has tithis from Pratipada through Purnima or Amavasya.

Ekadashi is the 11th tithi in each paksha and is often observed with a vrat. Purnima is the full-moon tithi, while Amavasya is the new-moon tithi. Fasting rules and puja customs can differ by family and tradition.

### Sunrise and Tithi End Time

A tithi can continue across part of two civil dates. A calendar may show the tithi at sunrise, the time it ends, sunrise and sunset, and sometimes moonrise. These details matter because many festivals and fasts are decided by the tithi present at a particular time.

| Calendar detail | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Tithi | The current lunar day | Identifies the day for a vrat or festival |
| Paksha | Bright or dark half of the month | Helps distinguish similar observances |
| Sunrise | The local start of the day for many rules | Used for many fasting decisions |
| Tithi end | The time the lunar day changes | Helps confirm the correct observance date |
| Parana | The allowed time to end a fast | Important after Ekadashi and other vrats |

## How Can You Check a Festival or Vrat Correctly?

Use a location-based panchang and check more than the large festival name. These steps give you a clearer answer than copying a date from a general calendar.

1. **Choose your city.** Select the place where you will observe the puja or fast. Time zones, sunrise and sunset can change the result.
2. **Find the festival or vrat.** Look for the full entry, not just the date shown in a search result or phone reminder.
3. **Check the tithi at sunrise.** This is often central to the festival rule, though some observances use moonrise, sunset or another specific time.
4. **Read the start and end times.** Note the local time at which the tithi changes.
5. **Check fasting and parana details.** Do not assume that a fast ends at midnight. Many fasts have a specific breaking period.
6. **Follow your family or regional custom.** A calendar gives the astronomical basis, while your tradition may guide the puja method, food rules and order of worship.

## Why Do Tithi Dates Differ Between Calendars?

Tithi dates can differ because calendars use different locations, sunrise times, regional traditions and methods for deciding the festival day. A tithi does not follow the fixed 24-hour clock, so a small time difference can move an observance to the previous or next civil date.

The month system can also matter. Many North Indian calendars use the month ending at Purnima, while many western and southern traditions use a month ending at Amavasya. The festival itself may still be widely recognised, but the month name or date shown beside it can differ.

A reliable calendar should make its location clear. It should also show whether a date is for the main puja, the start of a multi-day festival, a fasting day or the next morning's parana.

## How Do You Check Purnima, Chhath, Dev Uthani and Padwa Dates?

The right date depends on the festival rule, not only on the name of the lunar day. Here are useful examples of how to read these entries.

### Purnima in 2026

There is no single Purnima 2026 date. Each lunar month has its own full-moon tithi, so you need to choose the month and city. A calendar can also show the exact Purnima end time, which may be useful for Satyanarayan puja, bathing, charity or other family practices.

### Chhath Puja in 2026

The main Chhath Puja day in 2026 is commonly listed as 15 November. Chhath includes several days of observance, and the evening and morning arghya timings depend on local sunset and sunrise, so check the entry for your city rather than using only the date.

### Dev Uthani Gyaras in 2027

For the 2027 Dev Uthani Gyaras date, use a location-based calendar that shows Kartik Shukla Ekadashi and the sunrise rule. Published calendars can differ by a day, and the fasting break may fall at a different time from the main festival observance.

### Padwa in 2026

The phrase 2026 Padwa date needs some care because Padwa can mean different festivals. Gudi Padwa, linked with Chaitra Shukla Pratipada, is generally observed on 19 March 2026. Diwali Padwa is a separate observance, so check which regional festival you mean before saving the date.

## What Does Om Namo Narayanaya Mean?

Om Namo Narayanaya means Om, I bow to Narayana, or I offer reverence to Narayana. Narayana is a name of the Divine and is especially associated with Vishnu.

This is a devotional mantra, not a tithi label. A calendar or spiritual guide may include it alongside a Vishnu-related observance. You can repeat it with a calm mind and respect for your own tradition; there is no need to force a fixed count unless your teacher or family practice gives one.

## What Common Tithi Calendar Mistakes Should You Avoid?

The most common mistake is treating a tithi like a fixed date that begins at midnight. This can lead to the wrong day for a vrat or puja.

Also avoid these errors:

- Using a calendar set to another city or country
- Confusing the tithi start date with the festival date
- Ignoring sunrise, sunset or moonrise rules
- Breaking a fast without checking the parana window
- Assuming every region follows the same month system
- Treating a festival as a one-day event when it has several stages

If a date matters for a fast, wedding, temple visit or family puja, compare the calendar entry with the custom followed in your home. A clear panchang should give the times needed for that decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a tithi the same as a Gregorian date?

A tithi is a lunar day, while a Gregorian date is a civil calendar date. One tithi can cross midnight, so both dates may appear together in a panchang.

### Why can the same festival show different dates?

The date can change because of location, sunrise, regional tradition and the rule used for observing the festival. Some festivals are based on the tithi at sunrise, while others use sunset, moonrise or another time.

### How do I check a tithi for my city?

Choose your city or time zone in a reliable panchang, then check the tithi at sunrise and its end time. Read the festival or vrat notes as well, especially for fasting and parana.

### What is the difference between Purnima and Amavasya?

Purnima is the full-moon tithi, and Amavasya is the new-moon tithi. Both recur each lunar month and can have different festival, puja and fasting customs.
