24 September 2026 is a Thursday. Its Hindu tithi is Bhadrapada Shukla Trayodashi, the 13th lunar day of the waxing moon, and the evening is suitable for Pradosh worship where Trayodashi continues during the local sunset period. Exact tithi and puja timings depend on your city, so check a location-based panchang before starting a vrat.

## What Is the Day and Tithi on 24 September 2026?

The day is Thursday, and the tithi is Bhadrapada Shukla Trayodashi. This is also known as Shukla Paksha Trayodashi because it falls in the bright, or waxing, half of the lunar month.

| Panchang detail | Information |
|---|---|
| Gregorian date | 24 September 2026 |
| Weekday | Thursday |
| Lunar month | Bhadrapada |
| Paksha | Shukla Paksha |
| Tithi | Trayodashi, the 13th lunar day |
| Main evening observance | Pradosh puja, if Trayodashi is present at sunset |

A tithi does not begin and end at midnight like a normal calendar date. It is based on the distance between the Sun and Moon. This is why a tithi can start during the day, continue through the evening, and end the next morning.

In many Indian calendars, the Thursday Pradosh observance is called Guru Pradosh Vrat. The key rule is that Trayodashi should be present during the local Pradosh period, which is the part of the evening around sunset.

## What Does Bhadrapada Shukla Trayodashi Mean?

Trayodashi is the 13th tithi. Shukla Paksha is the period when the Moon is growing from Amavasya towards Purnima. Bhadrapada is the lunar month in which this Trayodashi falls in the calendar used for this date.

For many devotees, Shukla Trayodashi is a good time to pray to Bhagavan Shiva, slow down, and keep the evening focused on worship. A vrat does not have to be difficult to be sincere. Prayer, self-control, kindness, and a quiet mind are all meaningful parts of the observance.

## How Can You Observe Pradosh Puja at Home?

Begin by checking the Pradosh timing for your town. Try to complete the main puja during the local evening period when Trayodashi is active. If you cannot follow the full vrat, you can still offer a simple prayer at home.

### A simple home puja sequence

1. Take a bath and wear clean clothes before the evening puja.
2. Clean the place of worship and keep an image or Shiva linga in a suitable place.
3. Light a diya and offer water with a calm and respectful mind.
4. Offer bilva leaves, flowers, or fruit if these are part of your family practice. None of these should be treated as a test of devotion.
5. Chant Om Namah Shivaya, read a short Shiva prayer, or sit quietly in meditation.
6. Offer naivedya, perform aarti, and share the prasad with family members.

Water is enough for a simple abhishek. Milk, curd, honey, or other offerings are optional and should be used according to your tradition. Avoid waste and do not use any offering that your family custom does not support.

Some devotees keep a full fast, while others take fruit, milk, or one simple meal. Children, older adults, pregnant women, and people with health concerns should not force a fast. A prayerful meal and steady conduct are better than an unsafe fast.

### What should you pray for?

You may pray for inner steadiness, wisdom, health, and the strength to act with dharma. It is also a good time to remember family members, offer gratitude, and ask forgiveness for mistakes.

## Which Mantras and Aartis Can You Include?

Om Namah Shivaya is a simple and widely used Shiva mantra for this evening. If your family follows a Shiva Gayatri mantra, you may recite it from a trusted source or your own tradition. Popular versions differ in wording, so do not mix lines from different texts without checking them.

You can also include other prayers if they are part of your daily practice. They do not cancel the focus of Pradosh worship.

### Ganesha and Devi prayers

A Ganesha Gayatri mantra may be recited at the start of puja to seek clarity, good sense, and the removal of inner obstacles. For its meaning and a simple method of practice, see [Ganesha Gayatri Mantra: Meaning & Practice](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/ganesha-gayatri-mantra-meaning).

If your family sings Om Jai Ambe Gauri, you may include the Jai Ambe Gauri aarti during a home prayer session. It praises Devi as the compassionate Mother and asks for her care and guidance. You can read the [Jai Ambe Gauri Aarti: Lyrics and Meaning](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/jai-ambe-gauri-aarti-lyrics) if you want the Hindi words and an English explanation.

Ya Devi Sarva Bhuteshu is another well-known Devi prayer. Its central idea is that the Divine Mother is present in all beings in many forms, including awareness, strength, peace, and compassion. Regular recitation can support gratitude and awareness, but no mantra should be treated as a promise of a fixed worldly result.

## How Do You Check the Correct Local Timing?

Use a panchang that lets you choose your city. The tithi, sunrise, sunset, and Pradosh period can vary by location. A timing shown for Delhi may not be right for Mumbai, Bengaluru, London, Dubai, or another place.

Check these details before you plan the puja:

- The time Trayodashi begins and ends.
- Whether Trayodashi is present at your local sunset.
- The local sunset time and Pradosh period.
- The fasting start and end rule followed by your family.
- Any regional rule used by your temple or household.

## Is This Tithi Suitable for Every Auspicious Work?

A spiritually meaningful tithi is not automatically the right muhurat for every ceremony. Marriage, house entry, business openings, naming ceremonies, and other major events may require a wider check of nakshatra, yoga, karana, weekday, sunrise, and the local muhurat.

For daily prayer, Shiva worship, charity, reading, meditation, and a personal vrat, this evening can be observed with devotion. For a major family ceremony, follow the advice of a knowledgeable priest and your family tradition rather than judging the date from Trayodashi alone.
