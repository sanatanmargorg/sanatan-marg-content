## Where is Delhi's Tirupati Temple?

The Sri Venkateswara Temple in Delhi is at Sector 3, R.K. Puram, New Delhi 110022. It is a shrine of Lord Venkateswara, a form of Lord Vishnu also known as Balaji, Srinivasa, and Govinda. Check the latest temple schedule before leaving because worship services, holidays, and festival crowds can change access times.

## What can you expect at the shrine?

The temple offers a place for darshan, prayer, quiet reflection, and traditional worship. Depending on the time and day, you may see devotees taking part in aarti, offering prayers, or attending a seva. Some services may have separate rules, timings, or tickets, so ask at the temple office or counter.

### Why is the deity called Balaji?

Lord Venkateswara is worshipped as a compassionate form of Lord Vishnu. The names Balaji, Srinivasa, and Govinda are widely used by devotees, especially in the tradition connected with Tirumala in Andhra Pradesh. A visit does not need to be complicated: come with a calm mind, offer your prayer, and follow the temple's customs.

### What should a first-time visitor know?

Wear clean, modest clothing that is suitable for a place of worship. Keep your phone on silent, avoid loud conversation, and follow instructions about footwear, photography, food, and bags. Temple rules can change during busy days, so accept guidance from volunteers and staff.

## How should you plan darshan?

Plan for a little waiting time, especially on weekends, festival days, and Ekadashi observances. Do not rely on an old timing shown in a map listing or social media post. Use the latest notice from the temple or contact the temple before travelling.

1. Confirm the morning and evening darshan windows.
2. Check if a special seva or festival changes public entry.
3. Keep an extra 30 to 60 minutes for queues and security checks.
4. Carry only what you need, since large bags may not be allowed inside.
5. If you want prasadam or a particular offering, ask where it is available and how it should be offered.

| Visit detail | What to check |
|---|---|
| Darshan | Current opening, closing, and break timings |
| Seva | Registration, ticket, and reporting rules |
| Dress | Clean and modest clothing suitable for worship |
| Offerings | Temple-approved flowers, fruits, or other items |
| Photography | Areas where cameras and phones are restricted |
| Crowds | Extra time on weekends and important Vaishnava days |

The temple may close or limit entry during an internal ritual. This is normal in many Hindu temples. If you arrive during a break, wait peacefully or return during the next public darshan period.

## How do you reach the temple from across Delhi?

R.K. Puram is in South Delhi and is well connected by road, metro, bus, auto-rickshaw, and cab. The R.K. Puram Metro station on the Magenta Line can be useful for some visitors, but the final road distance depends on your starting point. Check the route on the day of travel and use the full locality, Sector 3, R.K. Puram, when setting your map destination.

If you are coming from the airport, New Delhi Railway Station, or another part of the city, traffic can make travel time change sharply. Leave more time during office hours and on festival days. Do not assume that a listing for another Balaji or South Indian temple in Delhi is the same shrine. Read the sector and address before starting your trip.

## Which prayers and mantras fit a visit?

You may pray in the language and form that feels sincere to you. A short Ganesha prayer can be used before setting out, while a Vishnu prayer can be recited during personal worship. Mantra practice is meant to steady the mind and deepen devotion; it should not be treated as a promise of a fixed worldly result.

### How can you use the Ganesha mantra?

The mantra **Om Gam Ganapataye Namaha** is traditionally offered to Lord Ganesha. Devotees recite it while asking for clarity, a steady mind, and the removal of inner obstacles before beginning an important task. You can repeat it a few times slowly before leaving home or while sitting quietly after darshan.

For a simple explanation of another Ganesha prayer, read [Ganesha Gayatri Mantra: Meaning & Practice](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/ganesha-gayatri-mantra-meaning). Pronunciation matters less than a respectful, focused practice, but learning the words carefully can help you recite with confidence.

### What does the Vishnu prayer mean?

A commonly recited Vishnu Gayatri is:

**Om Narayanaya Vidmahe Vasudevaya Dhimahi Tanno Vishnu Prachodayat.**

In simple terms, it is a prayer to know Narayana, meditate on Vasudeva, and ask Lord Vishnu to guide our understanding. It can be recited at home, before visiting the temple, or after darshan. You can also read [Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya: Benefits & Practice](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/om-namo-bhagavate-vasudevaya-benefits) for a related Vishnu mantra practice.

## Are Amarkantak and this Delhi temple the same place?

No. Amarkantak is a separate pilgrimage town in Madhya Pradesh, known for the source of the Narmada River and several important temples. The Narmada Udgam Temple is associated with Maa Narmada, while other shrines in Amarkantak honor deities such as Shiva. Do not use an Amarkantak address when planning a visit to the Delhi temple.

## What is the tithi on 23 August 2026?

For Delhi, 23 August 2026 is generally listed as Shukla Ekadashi, but check a Delhi-based panchang for the exact start and end time. Tithi timings can cross the sunrise boundary, and fasting or festival rules may follow the local panchang. If you plan to visit on that date, also confirm whether the temple has a special Ekadashi schedule or larger crowds.

## What mistakes should first-time visitors avoid?

A few small mistakes can make a temple visit harder than it needs to be:

- Arriving without checking the day's darshan and seva timings.
- Confusing the Sector 3 shrine with another South Indian temple in R.K. Puram or elsewhere in Delhi.
- Carrying large bags, outside food, or items that security may not permit.
- Taking photographs where the temple has restricted them.
- Treating a mantra as a quick guarantee instead of a prayerful practice.
- Starting a strict fast without considering your health or asking a trusted family elder or guide.

If you are visiting with children, older relatives, or someone who needs support, plan rest time and ask staff about the easiest entry route. A patient visit is usually more peaceful than trying to fit darshan into a very tight schedule.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Where is the temple in Delhi?

The shrine is in Sector 3, R.K. Puram, New Delhi 110022. Use the full name and locality in your map app because Delhi has more than one Balaji or South Indian temple.

### What are the darshan timings?

Darshan timings can change because of daily worship, sevas, holidays, and festivals. Check the latest temple notice or contact the temple before travelling instead of relying only on an old online listing.

### Is the Delhi shrine the same as the Amarkantak temple?

No, they are separate pilgrimage places. The Delhi shrine is in R.K. Puram, while Amarkantak is in Madhya Pradesh and is known for the Narmada's source and several different temples.

### Which mantra can I recite before darshan?

You may recite Om Gam Ganapataye Namaha for a calm beginning or the Vishnu prayer Om Narayanaya Vidmahe Vasudevaya Dhimahi Tanno Vishnu Prachodayat. Repeat either prayer slowly and respectfully, without treating it as a guaranteed promise of a particular result.
