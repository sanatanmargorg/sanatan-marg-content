Basant Panchami 2026 will be observed in India on Friday, 23 January 2026. The festival honours Maa Saraswati, the Hindu goddess associated with knowledge, learning, music and the arts. The exact Saraswati Puja muhurat depends on your city and the local Panchami tithi, so check a location-based panchang for the final time.

## When Is Basant Panchami 2026?

Basant Panchami falls on the fifth lunar day, or Panchami tithi, of the bright half of the Hindu month of Magha. In 2026, the festival falls on 23 January. Many families perform Saraswati Puja during the part of the day when Panchami tithi is present.

## What Does Basant Panchami Celebrate?

Basant Panchami marks the worship of Maa Saraswati and the value of knowledge. Students, teachers, musicians, writers and artists often pray for wisdom, focus, creativity and a good understanding of their work.

The word “Basant” refers to spring. In parts of India, the festival is linked with the change in season, fresh mustard flowers and the colour yellow. Yellow clothes, flowers and food are common in many homes, though customs differ from one region and family to another.

The festival is also seen as a good time to begin learning. Some families arrange a child’s first writing lesson, known in many places as Vidyarambh or Aksharabhyasam. Others place books, musical instruments, pens or study materials near the deity during the puja.

## How Can You Perform Saraswati Puja at Home?

You do not need a large setup to observe Basant Panchami with faith. A clean space, a respectful prayer and a sincere wish to use knowledge well are enough for a simple home puja.

### Prepare the puja space

Clean a small table or shelf and place a picture or murti of Maa Saraswati. You may add a clean cloth, yellow flowers, a lamp, incense, fruits and a simple offering. Books, notebooks, musical instruments or art tools may be placed nearby as a sign of respect for learning.

### Follow a simple worship order

1. Bathe and wear clean clothes. Yellow is traditional in many regions, but any clean and respectful clothing is suitable.
2. Clean the puja space and light a diya if you normally use one.
3. Offer flowers, fruits, water, rice, turmeric or sweets according to your family custom.
4. Pray to Maa Saraswati for wisdom, discipline, clear speech and the right use of knowledge.
5. Read a few pages of a sacred text, study material or a book connected with your learning.
6. Offer aarti or sit quietly for a few minutes before sharing the prasad.

You may recite a Saraswati prayer or mantra that your family already follows. There is no need to force a long ritual if you are new to the practice. A short prayer done with attention is better than copying steps without understanding them.

### Include children and students

Invite children to place their books near the puja area and say a short prayer before study. If your family follows the custom of beginning a child’s writing lessons on this day, use a slate, notebook or tray of rice. Let the child write a sacred syllable or the first letters of the alphabet under family guidance.

## What Are the Best Basant Panchami Offerings?

Offerings vary across India. Yellow rice, saffron-coloured sweets, boondi, fruits, kheer and seasonal foods are used in some homes. Other families keep the food simple and offer whatever is fresh, clean and suitable for the household.

A useful rule is to choose vegetarian food that your family traditionally offers and to prepare it with care. Keep a portion aside for prasad before the meal. Do not worry if you cannot find a particular flower, sweet or yellow ingredient. Devotion does not depend on one fixed menu.

## What Time Should You Perform Saraswati Puja?

The best time is the Saraswati Puja muhurat shown for your location while Panchami tithi is active. The exact period can change by city because Hindu calendar calculations use local sunrise, sunset and the beginning and end of the lunar tithi.

Avoid using a time copied from a different city without checking it. If you cannot perform the puja at the recommended time, pray during the same day and follow the guidance of your family priest or temple. The purpose of the muhurat is to support worship, not to create fear or stress.

## What Customs Should You Follow on Basant Panchami?

Basant Panchami customs are regional, so there is no single practice that every Hindu family must follow. Common observances include:

- Wearing yellow clothes or using yellow flowers in the puja.
- Worshipping books, instruments, writing tools and other objects linked with learning.
- Visiting a Saraswati temple or joining a community puja.
- Beginning a child’s first writing lesson.
- Studying, reading, singing or practising an art with a calm mind.
- Sharing prasad and helping a student, school or educational cause when possible.

Some parts of India also mark the day with kites, fairs or seasonal gatherings. These customs are meaningful in their local setting, but they are not required for a respectful home observance.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

The most common mistake is treating one printed puja time as correct for every place. Always check the city attached to a panchang. Another mistake is assuming that yellow clothes or a large offering are compulsory. They are familiar customs, not a measure of devotion.

Do not mix several regional rituals without knowing what they mean. Choose a simple method that your household understands. Also avoid turning the puja into a rushed photo session. Keep phones aside for a few minutes, give children a clear role and spend some quiet time in prayer or study.

Gudi Padwa 2027 is expected on 7 April, while Makar Sankranti is expected on 15 January 2027 because it follows the Sun’s movement into Makara.

Devutthani Ekadashi 2027 is expected in November, but the exact date and fasting rules should be checked in a local panchang. “Purnima 2026” is not one single festival date: every month has a Purnima, and some full-moon observances have different regional names and practices.

For all of these festivals, check the month, city and purpose before planning a fast or puja. A calendar may show the tithi on one date while the main observance is kept on another date because of sunrise or local religious custom.
