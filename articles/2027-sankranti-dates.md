## Why Does Sankranti Fall on Different Dates Each Month?

Sankranti marks the Sun's movement from one zodiac sign to the next in the sidereal solar calendar. This is why there is usually one Sankranti each month, unlike festivals based on the Moon, such as Purnima or Ekadashi.

Makar Sankranti is the best-known one because it marks the Sun's entry into Makara, or Capricorn. It is linked with longer daylight in the northern part of the year and is celebrated through different regional traditions, including Pongal, Magh Bihu, and Uttarayan. The names, foods, and customs may change, but the solar basis remains the same.

## How Should You Use the Sankranti Date and Time?

Use the date as a starting point, then check the Sankranti moment and local muhurat for your city. A printed calendar may show the time for another place, so it should not be copied without checking the location.

### Why does the city matter?

The Sun enters a zodiac sign at one exact astronomical moment, but clocks show different local times. If that moment falls near midnight, one location may observe the religious activities on one calendar date while another follows a nearby date according to local custom.

Many Panchangs also list Punya Kala, the period considered suitable for bathing, prayer, Surya worship, and charity. Some list Maha Punya Kala as a shorter preferred period. These timings are not the same everywhere.

### What if you do not know the exact time?

You can still observe the festival with faith on the date followed in your family or local Panchang. For important rituals, fasting, or charity, use a reliable city-based calendar and follow your family tradition rather than mixing times from several places.

## How Can You Observe Sankranti at Home?

A simple Sankranti observance can include a clean bath, a lamp, prayer to Surya, an offering of food, and charity. There is no need for an elaborate setup if you are observing at home for the first time.

### A simple home puja

1. Clean the prayer space and place a diya safely.
2. Take a bath and wear clean clothes.
3. Offer water to Surya in the morning if you can do so safely. Do not look directly at the Sun.
4. Offer flowers, fruits, sesame, jaggery, or a simple homemade dish.
5. Say a prayer, recite a mantra, and sit quietly for a few minutes.
6. Share food or give useful items in charity according to your means.

Sesame and jaggery are common in Makar Sankranti customs, while rice dishes, sugarcane, khichdi, and regional foods may also be part of the day. Food rules vary, so do not treat one regional practice as the only correct one.

## Which Mantras and Aartis Can You Recite?

You can recite a Surya prayer, a family deity mantra, or a short prayer you already know. The purpose is steady remembrance and gratitude, not the number of verses recited.

The mantra Om Namo Narayanaya means “I bow to Narayana” or “salutations to Narayana.” Narayana is a name of the Divine, commonly associated with Vishnu. You may repeat it quietly during prayer if it belongs to your family practice.

If your home worship includes Shiva, a Shiva aarti such as Jai Shiv Omkara may be included. Families who worship Devi may choose Jai Ambe Gauri; you can read its [lyrics and meaning here](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/jai-ambe-gauri-aarti-lyrics). Use a trusted text and follow the form of worship taught in your family or temple.

## Is Sankranti a Holiday in 2027?

A Sankranti observance is not automatically a public holiday everywhere in India. Makar Sankranti is widely celebrated, but the holiday status can vary by state, school, employer, and local government calendar. Other monthly Sankrantis are often observances rather than days off work.

If you are planning travel, school leave, or an office holiday around a Sankranti holiday in 2027, check the official calendar for your state or organisation. Do not assume that a festival date and a public holiday are always the same.

## How Is Sankranti Different From Purnima and Tithi?

Sankranti is based on the Sun's entry into a zodiac sign. Purnima means the full-moon day, and a tithi is a lunar day calculated from the positions of the Sun and Moon. These systems often appear together in a Hindu calendar, but they do not mark the same event.

If you are checking a Purnima date in 2026, use a lunar calendar for that year rather than the 2027 Sankranti list. A Purnima can occur on different civil dates in different places, just as a Sankranti ritual time can depend on location.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

A few simple checks can prevent most date and puja errors:

- Do not use the timing for a different city without checking the location.
- Do not assume every Sankranti is a national holiday.
- Do not confuse a Sankranti date with Purnima, Amavasya, or another tithi.
- Do not treat one region's food or fasting rule as a rule for every Hindu family.
- Do not postpone a simple prayer because you cannot arrange a large puja.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the date of Makar Sankranti in 2027?

Makar Sankranti will be observed on 15 January 2027 in India. Check a local Panchang for the exact transition time and the best period for bathing, prayer, or charity in your city.

### Are all Sankranti dates public holidays in 2027?

No. Holiday rules vary by state, school, employer, and the particular Sankranti observance; Makar Sankranti is widely observed, but it is not a single uniform national holiday in every place.

### Why can Sankranti times differ by city?

The Sun enters a zodiac sign at one astronomical moment, but each place has its own local clock and time zone. This can affect the displayed date and the local periods recommended for observance.

### Is Sankranti the same as Purnima?

No. Sankranti marks the Sun's entry into a new zodiac sign, while Purnima is the full-moon tithi. They follow different calendar calculations and can occur on separate dates.
