## When Is Amla Ekadashi 2026?

Amla Ekadashi 2026 falls on Friday, 27 February, in India. It is the Phalguna Shukla Ekadashi, observed through a fast, prayer to Lord Vishnu, and reverence for the amla tree; the fasting and parana times depend on your city and tradition. Check a local panchang for the sunrise-based tithi before making a strict plan.

The date of an Ekadashi is set by the lunar tithi, not by midnight on the civil calendar. This is why the start and end time may fall on different dates, and why parana times can vary between cities.

| Date | Main observance | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| 26 February 2026 | Dashami preparation | Keep the evening simple if your family tradition does so. |
| 27 February 2026 | Amla Ekadashi vrat | Fast according to your health, tradition, and local sunrise. |
| 28 February 2026 | Dwadashi parana | Break the fast after sunrise within the permitted local window. |

## What Does Amla Ekadashi Mean?

Amla Ekadashi is also called Amalaki Ekadashi. It comes in the bright half of the Hindu month of Phalguna and is linked with devotion to Vishnu and respect for the amla tree. Regional vrat kathas and customs differ, so your family or temple may follow a slightly different form of worship.

The day is not only about avoiding food. Devotees use it to reduce distractions, remember the Divine, practise self-control, and offer charity or service. A simple fast followed with sincere prayer is better than a difficult fast kept only as a test of endurance.

Many people observe the day for:

- Prayer, naam japa, and reading devotional texts.
- A clean and simple routine with less anger, gossip, and excess.
- Charity, feeding those in need, or helping someone in a practical way.
- Reflection on habits that they want to change.

## How Should You Observe the Vrat at Home?

You can observe Amla Ekadashi at home with a clean space, a clear intention, and a form of fasting that is safe for you. The exact puja steps are not the same in every region, so keep your family custom at the centre.

### Before sunrise

Rise early, bathe, and clean the place where you pray. Place an image or murti of Vishnu if you use one, light a lamp, and make a simple sankalpa for the vrat. Decide your fasting level before the day begins instead of changing it to compete with someone else.

If you are pregnant, elderly, unwell, diabetic, taking regular medicine, or recovering from illness, do not attempt a strict fast without medical advice. Devotion does not require you to harm your health.

### During the day

Offer water, flowers, a lamp, and prayers according to your practice. Tulsi may be included if it is part of your Vishnu puja custom. Some devotees also offer or keep amla fruit as a respectful part of the day, but it is not necessary to copy a ritual that is not followed in your home.

You may chant Vishnu names, read a familiar devotional text, visit a temple, or spend quiet time in prayer. Keep the mood peaceful. If you cannot attend a temple, sincere worship at home is still meaningful.

### Prayer and seva

Amla Ekadashi is a good time for simple seva. You might donate food, support a gaushala or community kitchen, help an elderly person, or give time to someone who needs it. Choose an act that you can do with respect and without seeking praise.

## What Can You Eat During the Fast?

There is no single food rule followed by every Hindu family. Some devotees keep a nirjala fast without water, while others take water, milk, fruit, nuts, or one light meal. Many Ekadashi traditions avoid rice, wheat, other grains, and pulses, but the list of permitted ingredients can differ by region and household.

Common vrat foods may include fruit, milk, nuts, sabudana, kuttu, rajgira, or samak, where these are accepted by your tradition. Read the ingredients of packaged foods carefully, since seasonings and flour mixes may contain items your vrat avoids.

Amla fruit can be eaten only if it fits your chosen fasting rule and suits your health. The name of the festival does not mean you must eat amla. The spirit of the day is devotion and restraint, not a fixed menu.

## When Should You Break the Amla Ekadashi Fast?

Parana means breaking the Ekadashi fast. For Amla Ekadashi 2026, parana is generally observed on Saturday, 28 February, after sunrise and within the Dwadashi parana window shown by your local panchang.

Do not use a random time from a different city. The window can change with location, sunrise, and the details of the tithi. Some calendars also show a period that should be avoided before parana. Check the full local information before eating.

Start gently with water, fruit, or a light meal if that suits you. Then return to your normal food gradually. If your health or medication requires you to eat at a particular time, follow medical advice rather than forcing a religious timetable.

## How Can You Avoid Common Vrat Mistakes?

A few small checks can make the observance calmer and more accurate:

1. **Do not treat midnight as the start of Ekadashi.** The tithi is connected with the lunar calculation and local sunrise.
2. **Do not copy parana times from another city.** Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, London, and other places can have different sunrise-based timings.
3. **Do not force nirjala fasting.** A safe fruit, water, or light-food vrat is better than risking your health.
4. **Check ingredients in vrat foods.** Fasting snacks can contain grains, pulses, or seasonings that your custom avoids.
5. **Do not forget Dwadashi.** Breaking the fast at the proper local time is part of the observance.
6. **Do not judge another devotee's method.** Hindu families follow different rules for food, prayer, and the choice of Ekadashi calendar.

The next Amavasya depends on the date you are checking and the place where you live. Sankranti dates follow the Sun's movement, so their calculation is different from an Ekadashi. For an annual Ekadashi list, check whether the calendar states its location and whether it follows Smarta, Vaishnava, or another local observance.
