## What Are the Sankashti Chaturthi 2026 Dates?

Sankashti Chaturthi is observed every lunar month on Krishna Paksha Chaturthi, the fourth day after the full moon. In India, commonly used Panchang calendars list the 2026 observances on the dates below. The exact vrat date and moonrise time can differ by city, so check your local Panchang before making a firm plan.

| Month | Common India date | Note |
|---|---:|---|
| January | 6 January | Tuesday; Angarki Sankashti |
| February | 5 February | Monthly Sankashti |
| March | 6 March | Monthly Sankashti |
| April | 5 April | Monthly Sankashti |
| May | 4 May | Monthly Sankashti |
| June | 3 June | Monthly Sankashti |
| July | 2 July | Monthly Sankashti |
| July | 31 July | Additional lunar-month observance |
| August | 30 August | Monthly Sankashti |
| September | 28 September | Monthly Sankashti |
| October | 28 October | Monthly Sankashti |
| November | 26 November | Monthly Sankashti |
| December | 26 December | Monthly Sankashti |

The two July entries are not a mistake. Some 2026 Hindu calendars include an additional lunar-month observance, so devotees may see 13 Sankashti dates instead of the usual 12.

For each date, follow the tithi and moonrise timings for your location. A date shown in one city may change in another if Chaturthi begins or ends near midnight or if the moonrise falls at a different time.

## Why Do Hindus Observe This Vrat?

Sankashti means relief from difficulty. Devotees worship Lord Ganesha, who is honoured as the remover of obstacles and the giver of wisdom, steadiness and good beginnings. The vrat is a chance to slow down, pray with focus and seek strength for duties at home, work and study.

The fast is traditionally kept during the day and completed after seeing the moon. Many families also read a Sankashti vrat story, sing a Ganesh aarti and spend a few quiet minutes in prayer. The value of the observance lies in sincerity, not in making the ritual difficult.

## How Do You Perform Sankashti Chaturthi Puja?

Keep the puja simple and clean. You do not need a large setup or many offerings. A picture or murti of Lord Ganesha, a diya, water, flowers and a small offering are enough for a home observance.

### Before the puja

1. Wake up, bathe and wear clean clothes.
2. Clean the puja space and place Ganesha on a clean cloth.
3. Make a simple sankalp, or personal resolve, for the vrat.
4. Light a diya and offer water, flowers, durva grass, fruits or a modak if available.
5. Chant a Ganesh mantra and read or listen to the Sankashti vrat katha.

You can perform the main puja before moonrise and keep the final offering for the time when the moon is visible. Customs differ among families, so follow the practice you have received from your home or teacher.

### At moonrise

Check the local moonrise time rather than relying on a fixed time printed for another city. Offer arghya by raising a small vessel of water toward the moon, then offer a prayer to Ganesha. After this, complete the puja and break the fast according to your family tradition.

Some devotees offer water to the moon, while others simply join their hands and pray. Both can be done with respect. The key point is to avoid breaking the vrat before the proper moonrise if your tradition follows the usual Sankashti rule.

## What Can You Eat During the Fast?

Fasting rules differ across regions and families. Some devotees keep a strict fast until moonrise. Others take water, milk, fruit, nuts or a simple vrat meal made with permitted ingredients. Many avoid grains, pulses, onion, garlic and ordinary salt during the fast, but local customs are not the same everywhere.

Choose a form of fasting that is safe for your body. Pregnant people, older adults, children and anyone with a health condition should not force a strict fast. A prayerful, lighter observance is better than harming your health. If you take regular medicine or have been advised not to fast, follow your doctor’s guidance.

## Which Mantras and Aartis Can You Include?

A simple mantra is often the best choice when you want to keep the mind steady. You can repeat Om Gan Ganapataye Namah slowly for a few minutes, or read a longer prayer such as a Ganesh stotra or Ganesh Gayatri. The [Ganesha Gayatri mantra guide](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/ganesha-gayatri-mantra-meaning) explains its meaning and gives a useful way to practise it.

The [Jai Ganesh Jai Ganesh Deva aarti](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/jai-ganesh-aarti-lyrics-meaning) also fits naturally after Ganesh puja. Sing it with attention instead of worrying about speed or the number of repetitions.

Families may include other prayers during the day, but each aarti has its own deity and purpose. Kunj Bihari Ki is a Krishna aarti. Ambaji Aarti and Jai Ambe Gauri are forms of Devi worship. Om Namo Narayanaya is a salutation to Lord Narayana, while Om Shri Hanumate Namah is a prayer to Lord Hanuman. These prayers may be part of your wider daily worship, but they do not replace the central Ganesh focus of Sankashti Chaturthi.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid?

A few simple checks can prevent confusion:

- Do not use a date from another city without checking the local tithi and moonrise.
- Do not assume that sunrise alone decides the end of the vrat.
- Do not break the fast early if your family tradition completes it after moonrise.
- Do not copy a strict fasting rule that your health cannot support.
- Do not feel that expensive offerings are needed for the puja.
- Do not treat a mantra as a test of pronunciation. Learn the words carefully and pray with a calm mind.

If you miss a ritual step, continue respectfully. Hindu worship can be simple, and a sincere prayer at home has value.
