## What Are the Om Jai Shiv Omkara Lyrics?

Om Jai Shiv Omkara is a traditional Shiva aarti sung in praise of Lord Shiva. Its well-known opening is “Om Jai Shiv Omkara, Swami Jai Shiv Omkara”; the verses describe Shiva’s forms, symbols, companions, and grace.

A common Roman-script version is given below. Printed prayer books may use small spelling or wording differences, so do not worry if your family version is not exactly the same. Sing the opening refrain after each stanza.

**Refrain**

Om Jai Shiv Omkara, Swami Jai Shiv Omkara  
Brahma, Vishnu, Sadashiv, Ardhangi Dhara

**Stanza 1**

Ekanan, Chaturanan, Panchanan Raje  
Hansasan, Garudasan, Vrishavahan Saje

**Stanza 2**

Do Bhuj, Char Chaturbhuj, Dashabhuj Ati Sohe  
Trigun Roop Nirakhate, Tribhuvan Jan Mohe

**Stanza 3**

Akshamala, Vanamala, Mundamala Dhari  
Chandan Mrigamad Sohe, Bhale Shashi Dhari

**Stanza 4**

Shvetambar, Pitambar, Baghambar Ange  
Sankadik, Brahmadik, Bhootadik Sange

**Stanza 5**

Kar Men Madhya Kamandalu, Chakra Trishul Dharta  
Jagakarta, Jagrata, Jagpalankarta

**Stanza 6**

Brahma, Vishnu, Sadashiv, Janat Aviveka  
Pranavakshar Ke Madhya, Ye Tinon Eka

**Stanza 7**

Trigun Swami Ki Aarti, Jo Koi Nar Gave  
Kahat Shivanand Swami, Manvanchhit Phal Pave

The final line is traditionally understood as a prayer for the devotee’s sincere wishes to be fulfilled. It is best treated as a devotional hope, not as a promise of a reward.

## What Do the Main Verses Mean?

The aarti praises Shiva as the one reality honoured through different divine forms. It also points to familiar images from Shiva worship, such as the crescent moon, the trident, the sacred ash, and the tiger skin.

| Aarti image or phrase | Simple meaning | Main idea |
|---|---|---|
| Brahma, Vishnu, Sadashiv | The three divine functions are held together in the Supreme | Unity in the Divine |
| Ekanan, Chaturanan, Panchanan | One-faced, four-faced, and five-faced forms | Shiva’s many forms |
| Vrishavahan | Shiva rides the bull Nandi | Strength, steadiness, and devotion |
| Chandan, Mrigamad, Shashi | Sandal paste, musk, and the moon are linked with Shiva | Sacred beauty and calm |
| Kamandalu, Chakra, Trishul | Sacred objects and weapons held by divine forms | Protection and spiritual power |
| Pranavakshar | The sacred sound Om | The unity behind creation |

Some lines bring Brahma, Vishnu, and Sadashiva together. This does not need to be read as a competition between deities. Many Hindu traditions use such verses to express that the Divine can be worshipped through more than one form.

## How Do You Sing This Shiva Aarti at Home?

You can sing Om Jai Shiv Omkara during a simple daily puja or at the close of a longer Shiva worship. No elaborate setup is required. A clean place, a steady mind, and respectful prayer are enough.

1. Wash your hands and sit in a clean place before a Shiva murti, picture, or symbol.
2. Light a diya if it is safe to do so. Incense, flowers, water, and bilva leaves may be offered according to your family practice.
3. Begin with a quiet prayer. You may chant Om or remember Lord Shiva in your own words.
4. Sing the refrain and each stanza at a comfortable pace. Repeat the refrain after every stanza.
5. Move the lit diya in gentle clockwise circles during the aarti, keeping the flame away from loose clothing, children, and curtains.
6. Sit quietly for a moment after the song and offer your thanks.

Aarti is not a singing test. If you are learning, read one stanza at a time and listen to an elder or teacher in your family. Understanding the words will make the prayer more meaningful than rushing through every line.

## How Can You Pronounce the Aarti Clearly?

Roman spelling cannot show every sound in Hindi or Sanskrit, and regional singing styles differ. Clear, slow pronunciation is more helpful than trying to copy a fast tune.

### Useful pronunciation points

- **Jai** sounds close to “jay.”
- **Shiv** has a short vowel sound, close to “shiv,” not “sheeve.”
- **Omkara** means the sacred sound Om; say it as “Om-kaa-ra.”
- **Ardhangi** refers to the feminine half or consort understood as part of Shiva’s divine form.
- **Vrishavahan** refers to one whose vehicle is the bull, a reference to Nandi.
- **Pranavakshar** points to the sacred syllable Om.

Listen for the beat set by the person leading the prayer. The same aarti may sound different in a temple, home, or regional tradition. That difference is normal.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid During the Aarti?

The most common problem is not a wrong tune. It is treating a devotional prayer like a race or a fixed performance. Keep the practice simple and safe.

- Do not feel forced to use every listed offering. Offer only what you can arrange with care.
- Do not leave a diya or incense burning without someone present.
- Do not assume that one Roman transliteration is the only correct version.
- Do not mix unrelated lines into the aarti if you are trying to learn its traditional form.
- Do not focus so much on pronunciation that you lose the feeling of prayer.
- Do not promise a fixed worldly result to another devotee. Aarti is an act of devotion, gratitude, and surrender.

If you cannot sing, reading the words slowly or listening respectfully is also a valid way to take part in family worship.

## How Does This Aarti Relate to Tithis and Other Prayers?

Om Jai Shiv Omkara can be sung on any day. It does not require a special tithi, though many devotees choose extra Shiva worship on days that matter in their family or local tradition.

Questions about **purnima 2026**, **sankranti dates 2027**, or a **tithi calendar** are separate calendar questions. Tithi timings can differ by location and time zone, so check a trusted local panchang before planning a festival puja. A date shown for one Indian city may not match the timing in another city or in another country.

The mantra **Om Namo Narayanaya** is different from this Shiva aarti. It means “I bow to Narayana” or “salutations to Lord Narayana,” and it is widely used in Vaishnava worship. **Jaya Ambe Gauri** is a Devi aarti, sung in praise of the Divine Mother; you can read its [lyrics and meaning here](https://sanatanmarg.org/blog/jai-ambe-gauri-aarti-lyrics).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Om Jai Shiv Omkara?

Om Jai Shiv Omkara is a traditional Hindu aarti dedicated to Lord Shiva. It praises Shiva’s divine forms, symbols, and grace, and is commonly sung during puja.

### Can I sing this aarti every day?

Yes, you can sing it every day if it supports your devotional practice. No special tithi is required, though some families choose particular days for longer Shiva worship.

### What is the meaning of Om Namo Narayanaya?

Om Namo Narayanaya means “I bow to Narayana” or “salutations to Lord Narayana.” It is a devotional mantra associated with worship of Lord Vishnu and is separate from the Shiva aarti.

### Do I need a special tithi to perform this aarti?

No, Om Jai Shiv Omkara can be performed on any day. Festival and tithi timings may add meaning for some families, but they are not required for a sincere daily aarti.
