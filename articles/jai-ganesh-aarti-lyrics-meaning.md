## What Are the Jai Ganesh Jai Ganesh Deva Lyrics?

The familiar “Jai Ganesh Jai Ganesh Deva” is a Hindu aarti for Lord Ganesha. Its opening is: “Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh Deva / Mata Jaki Parvati, Pita Mahadeva.” Below is a widely sung Romanised version, followed by its meaning and a simple way to recite it at home.

Small wording changes are common because this aarti is sung in different regions and families. The version below uses **mooshak** for Ganesha’s mouse vehicle; some books write the word as **muse** or **mooshak**.

> Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh Deva  
> Mata Jaki Parvati, Pita Mahadeva  
> 
> Ek dant dayavant, char bhuja dhari  
> Mathe sindoor sohe, mooshak ki savari  
> 
> Andhan ko aankh det, kodhin ko kaya  
> Banjhan ko putra det, nirdhan ko maya  
> 
> Paan chadhe, phool chadhe, aur chadhe meva  
> Ladduan ka bhog lage, sant kare seva  
> 
> Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh Deva  
> Mata Jaki Parvati, Pita Mahadeva

The refrain is usually sung after each stanza. You may sing the whole aarti in this form or follow the version used by your family or temple.

## What Does This Ganesh Aarti Mean?

The aarti praises Ganesha as the son of Parvati and Mahadeva, or Lord Shiva. It describes his one tusk, four arms, vermilion mark, and mouse vehicle. The later lines present traditional prayers for relief, health, family well-being, and support for people facing hardship.

| Aarti line or idea | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh, Jai Ganesh Deva | Victory and praise to Lord Ganesha. |
| Mata Jaki Parvati, Pita Mahadeva | His mother is Parvati and his father is Shiva. |
| Ek dant, char bhuja dhari | He is shown with one tusk and four arms. |
| Mathe sindoor sohe, mooshak ki savari | Vermilion adorns him, and the mouse is his vehicle. |
| Paan, phool, meva and laddus | Devotees offer leaves, flowers, fruits, sweets, and service. |

The line about offering laddus reflects a familiar form of Ganesha worship. The value of the offering is not its price. A simple flower, fruit, or heartfelt prayer can be offered with respect.

## How Do You Pronounce the Aarti Clearly?

You do not need a perfect accent to sing this prayer. Read the Romanised words slowly, keep the beat steady, and learn one stanza at a time. Clear and sincere recitation is better than rushing through every line.

### Useful pronunciation help

- **Ganesh**: guh-naysh
- **Deva**: day-va
- **Parvati**: par-va-tee
- **Mahadeva**: ma-haa-day-va
- **Dayavant**: da-ya-vant
- **Savari**: sa-va-ree
- **Seva**: say-va

In some parts of India, the same words may sound slightly different. That is normal. You can also listen to the tune used at home or in your temple and follow that rhythm.

## How Can You Sing Jai Ganesh Aarti at Home?

You can sing this aarti during a daily puja, after offering prayers to Ganesha, or at the end of a special worship service. There is no need for a large setup.

### A simple home practice

1. Clean a small prayer space and place a picture or murti of Lord Ganesha if you wish.
2. Sit calmly and remember Ganesha as the remover of obstacles and giver of wisdom.
3. Light a diya or incense only if it is safe, especially around children, pets, and loose clothing.
4. Offer a flower, fruit, durva grass, or another simple item according to your family practice.
5. Sing the opening refrain, then continue with the stanzas. Repeat the refrain after each one.
6. End with a short prayer for clarity, good conduct, and the well-being of all.

You can sing alone or with family members. If you are short on time, repeat the opening refrain with attention rather than treating the prayer as a task to finish quickly. Aarti is an act of offering, gratitude, and remembrance.

## Can You Add Other Mantras to This Practice?

Yes. Ganesha aarti can be part of a wider prayer routine, but you do not need to recite many mantras at once. Choose a small practice that you can do with care.

### Shiva Gayatri

A commonly recited Shiva Gayatri is:

> Om Tatpurushaya Vidmahe  
> Mahadevaya Dhimahi  
> Tanno Rudrah Prachodayat

It is a prayer for awareness, devotion, and right guidance through Lord Shiva. You may recite it after remembering Ganesha, especially before study, meditation, or a quiet period of prayer.

### Ya Devi Sarva Bhuteshu

The well-known Devi prayer begins:

> Ya Devi Sarva Bhuteshu  
> Shakti-rupena Samsthita  
> Namastasyai, Namastasyai,  
> Namastasyai Namo Namah

It honours the Divine Mother as the power present in all beings. The spiritual benefit is best understood as remembrance of divine presence, strength, and compassion. It should not be treated as a promise of a fixed material result.

## How Should You Check a Tithi for a Specific Date?

A Hindu tithi depends on the positions of the Sun and Moon, the place of observation, and local sunrise. So the tithi shown for 24 September 2026, or for 23 August 2026, may not be the same in every city. A tithi can also begin or end during the day.

For questions such as the 24 September 2026 day tithi, the 24th September 2026 tithi, or the 23rd August 2026 tithi, use a location-based panchang. Check the city, sunrise time, and the period given for the tithi before planning a fast, puja, or festival observance.

## What Mistakes Should You Avoid During the Aarti?

A few simple habits make the practice more respectful and easier to maintain:

- Do not worry if your pronunciation is not perfect on the first day.
- Do not assume every printed version has exactly the same words.
- Do not make promises about guaranteed wealth, health, or success from the aarti.
- Do not force a long ritual when a short, sincere prayer is more suitable.
- Keep lamps and incense in a safe place, and put them out properly after worship.
- If you are unsure about a family or temple custom, ask an elder or priest rather than guessing.

The heart of the aarti is praise, humility, and gratitude. Regular practice matters more than a costly offering or elaborate arrangement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

The aarti praises Lord Ganesha as the son of Parvati and Shiva, describes his form and mouse vehicle, and asks for grace, relief, and well-being.
