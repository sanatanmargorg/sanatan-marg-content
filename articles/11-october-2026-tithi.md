11 October 2026 is Sunday and falls on Ashwin Shukla Pratipada, the first tithi of the bright fortnight. It is the first day of Sharad Navratri in the usual Indian calendar, and many families observe Ghatasthapana on this day. The exact puja window depends on your city, sunrise, and local panchang.

## What Is the Tithi on 11 October 2026?

The tithi on 11 October 2026 is **Shukla Pratipada**, the first day of Shukla Paksha in the month of Ashwin. Shukla Paksha is the waxing half of the lunar month, moving from the new moon towards the full moon.

This date has special importance because it begins Sharad Navratri, the nine-night worship period devoted to Devi in many Hindu traditions. In Gujarat, the same day may be called **Aso Sud Padvo**. Regional names and customs may differ, but the lunar tithi is the same basis for the observance.

Many Navadurga traditions associate the first day with Maa Shailaputri. Some families follow a different form of Devi or begin with their family deity. There is room for regional practice within the wider festival.

## Why Do Tithi Timings Change by City?

A tithi is not the same as a calendar date. It is based on the changing distance between the Sun and Moon, so it can begin or end at any time of day. It does not start at midnight in the way a normal date does.

For religious observance, panchangs commonly show which tithi is present at sunrise and give the suitable muhurta for a ceremony. This is why a Ghatasthapana time in Delhi may not be identical to one in Mumbai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Dubai, London, or New York.

Use these details when checking the timing:

1. Select your city or nearest town.
2. Confirm the local sunrise time.
3. Check the tithi at sunrise and the Pratipada period.
4. Follow the Ghatasthapana or puja muhurta given by a trusted local panchang.
5. If you follow a family priest, temple, or regional calendar, give that guidance priority for the ceremony.

Do not copy a timing from another city without checking the location. The tithi name may remain the same while the start, end, or puja window changes.

## How Can You Observe the First Day of Navratri?

You can observe the first day with a full Ghatasthapana ceremony or with a simple home prayer. The most important parts are a clean prayer space, a sincere intention, and respect for your family tradition.

### Ghatasthapana at home

Families commonly clean the puja area, place a kalash, add water and auspicious items, and invoke Devi. Many also place grains or soil in a small vessel and watch them grow through Navratri. A lamp may be kept for the festival if this is part of your household practice.

The exact items vary by region. Some homes use mango leaves, a coconut, red cloth, flowers, rice, or a small image of Devi. You do not need to copy every custom from another region. Use clean materials and follow the method known to your family.

### A simple prayer if you cannot perform Ghatasthapana

If you are working, travelling, living in a hostel, or unable to arrange a kalash, you can still mark the day in a simple way:

- Clean a small space and place an image of Devi.
- Light a lamp if it is safe and suitable for your home.
- Offer water, flowers, fruit, or another simple naivedya.
- Say a prayer or recite a Devi stotra you know.
- Take a quiet moment for gratitude, courage, and self-discipline.

Fasting is optional and differs across families. Some people take only fruit or one simple meal, while others avoid particular grains or ingredients. Do not fast in a way that harms your health. Children, older adults, pregnant women, and people with medical needs should follow suitable advice rather than a strict rule.

## Which Mantras and Readings Fit This Day?

A Navratri prayer may include a Devi mantra, Durga stuti, Devi Mahatmya reading, or a family prayer. You do not need to recite a long text to observe the day well. A few minutes of steady prayer can be more useful than rushing through words you do not understand.

If you begin a puja with Ganesha, the Ganesha Gayatri is a familiar choice. Its meaning asks for knowledge of the one-tusked Lord, meditation on the curved-trunk form, and guidance for the mind. ## What Is the Tithi on 24 September 2026?

24 September 2026 is Thursday. In many Indian panchangs, it is Shukla Trayodashi at sunrise, with the tithi changing to Chaturdashi later in some locations. The exact transition depends on the city and local time, so a city-specific panchang is needed for a firm answer.

This date falls close to the end of the bright fortnight before the September full moon. It is not the start of Sharad Navratri. That observance begins on 11 October 2026 with Ashwin Shukla Pratipada.

Checking both the date and tithi matters. A date can be the same across India while the tithi boundary or a ritual window changes during the day. This is also why a calendar may show more than one tithi for a single civil date.

## Can a Temple Visit Fit with This Tithi?

Yes, you may plan a temple visit on this day or during Navratri, but the tithi does not set the opening or darshan schedule of every temple. Temple timings, special pujas, crowd rules, and travel conditions must be checked with the temple or its local authority.

If you are planning a visit to **Neelkanth Mahadev in Pauri Garhwal**, check the local route, weather, road conditions, temple hours, and any festival arrangements before leaving. A personal prayer at the temple can be part of your observance, while the Navratri Ghatasthapana muhurta should still be checked for your location.
